# Why v3 is trained this way

v3 fine-tunes Llama 3.2 3B Instruct with 4-bit QLoRA on a Constitutional AI mix drawn from the twenty English public-domain books with the highest Gorsuch and McPherson I/E-R intrinsic scores. The question is whether that high-intrinsic slice, versus a citation-matched public-domain control of the same size, moves how the model answers held-out dilemmas. I/E-R scores are trainer metadata: they pick books and set row weights. They never appear in prompts, critiques, or completions. Critique and revision stay on the authors’ own vocabulary, in the Constitutional AI sense of Bai et al. (2022, arXiv:2212.08073): a principle is an instruction for a critique, not a passage the model is asked to imitate.

## A hard quality cut, then one weighed turn

v1 trained on the full forty-book dialogues. v2 kept those forty and mixed in extra contemporary rows. v3 instead keeps ranks 1–20 of the I/E-R list in [`four-top40-lists.md`](../four-top40-lists.md), from Brother Lawrence through John Taylor. Ranking is by I/E-R intrinsic; I−E is not a second gate, because the published scales treat intrinsic as its own construct.

The easy mistake is to keep the noisier tail and downweight it. Zhou et al. (2023, LIMA, arXiv:2305.11206) and Chen et al. (2023, AlpaGasus, arXiv:2307.08701) found a small curated slice beat the larger set. A low-scoring row still contributes gradient: the optimizer sees it every epoch, even at a small weight. Downweighting therefore leaves the noise in the mix. A hard cut removes it, and weights then vary only among already-high books. That is also why each kept row is shown once per epoch. Resampling with replacement, as v2 did, lets a lucky draw duplicate a row and starve another, which turns the curated set back into an uneven sample of itself. The 1,001 CAI rows in [`colab/data/v3/mix_summary.json`](../../colab/data/v3/mix_summary.json) are how many v2 traces survived the cut.

Each kept v2 `draft_0` / `revision_3` trace is rewritten, with `deepseek-flash`, into one user dilemma and one weighed assistant reply. If the whole critique-and-revision transcript were the completion, the loss would teach book diction and the critique ritual along with the answer. Guan et al. (2024, arXiv:2412.16339) treat the saved completion as what a careful person would *say after* weighing a principle: it states a prior, names the principle as a candidate, and says what it changes and what it does not. Loss is on that completion only. The generation may reason in a short private exchange; that draft is discarded, so the 3B model is not trained to imitate the book.

The mix that enters the trainer is those rewritten CAI rows plus a shared 300-row format-replay slice (ARC-Easy train items and synthetic letter-format items), every replay row at weight 1. That is the InstructGPT pattern (Ouyang et al., 2022). The primary metric is 8-token generation plus `parse_letter`. After an epoch of long deliberative completions, a 3B model can answer a multiple-choice item with a paragraph that never contains `A`–`D`, and the scorer marks it wrong. A little letter-format data keeps the output shape the scorer expects. The published files are 1,001 CAI rows per arm plus those 300 replay rows (1,301 SFT and 1,301 DPO).

## Weights, and a uniform arm on the same rows

After the filter, Arm A sets `w = s / mean(s_kept)` with no clip, then renormalizes so the mean CAI weight is 1. Byrd and Lipton (2019) and Cui et al. (2019) are why the score is a loss weight rather than a sampling probability, and why the weights stay uncapped. Sampling by score would redraw the slice. Clipping would flatten the top of a distribution whose heaviest kept weight is already 2.248. Uncapped weights raise variance, so the run logs Kish effective sample size `(∑w)² / ∑w²`. For this mix that ESS is 789.94 on 1,001 rows; the heaviest CAI weight is 2.248 and the lightest is 0.571 — real work, not a collapse onto a handful of books.

Arm B is the same 1,001 rows and the same row ids, every weight 1. The hard cut and the weights are two different claims. If the only contrast were weighted high-intrinsic versus control, a gain could come from having kept the twenty books, from having weighted them, or from both. Arm B holds the filter fixed and turns the weights off. At a given seed, A and B share the shuffle so `w` is the only difference.

Arm C draws 1,001 control dialogues, stratified by book so one author cannot fill the set, all weights 1, plus the same 300 replay rows. Size-matching is what makes A minus C a corpus contrast rather than “more rows versus fewer.”

When evaluation exists, the reported comparison is the base model, Arm C, Arm B, and Arm A. WiSE-FT λ=0.5 is the reported operating point for the three trained arms; λ=0 is the base, scored once; λ=1 on Arm A is a reference for the unblended adapter.

## Length, both kinds of rejection, and three seeds

Preference training is R-DPO (Park, Rafailov, Ermon, and Finn, 2024, arXiv:2403.19159) with α=0.01 and β=0.1, the values they report for the Helpful-Harmless dialogue setting at this β. Ordinary DPO’s implicit reward tracks completion length: a longer chosen answer looks more preferred, so the trainer learns to ramble. A cleanup after the fact — drop long answers, or add a second penalty — would change which pairs the arms see, or split the objective. Park put the length term *inside* the logistic, so a longer chosen answer shrinks that pair’s gradient in the same update. β=0.1 is also the DPO default for staying near the reference (Rafailov et al., 2023, arXiv:2305.18290). The same R-DPO loss is used on every arm.

Rejected answers alternate between the unrevised draft (501 pairs) and a dogmatic rewrite that drops the user’s constraints (500 pairs), on every arm. If the rejected answer were always the draft, the preference would say “move as far as you can from the first try.” That trains maximum movement: a model that always revises hard can look aligned on the principle and still steamroll the user. Sharma et al. (2023, arXiv:2310.13548) treat sycophancy as bidirectional — ignore the principle, or parrot it and run over the person who asked — so both rejected types are in the mix. Chosen is always the weighed, deliberative completion.

Dodge et al. (2020, arXiv:2002.06305) is why there are three seeds, 0, 1, and 2, and why the reported number is their mean. Initialization and data order each move fine-tuning results by as much as a real treatment effect; picking the best seed would write that noise down as a finding.

Every arm, every seed, uses the same QLoRA setup (Dettmers et al., 2023; Hu et al., 2021): all seven projections, rank 16, alpha 32, one SFT epoch at `1e-4`, then R-DPO at `5e-6`. The row weight multiplies the whole per-sequence SFT loss and the whole per-pair R-DPO loss once per epoch. Adapters write under `adapters/{ACTIVE_MODEL}/v3/`.

## How scores will be read

Wortsman et al. (2022, WiSE-FT, arXiv:2109.01903) interpolate the fine-tuned weights with the base: `(1−λ) θ_base + λ θ_ft`. Fine-tuning improves the training distribution and often hurts everything else. λ=0.5 is a blend of those two functions, the operating point their paper found often keeps most of the in-distribution gain while recovering the base’s robustness — not a hedge in case the adapter is wrong. λ=1 on Arm A is a reference for the unblended adapter. λ=0 is the base, scored once. The primary metric remains 8-token generation plus `parse_letter`. Dror et al. (2018) is why the interval will be a paired bootstrap over items on Arm A minus Arm B and Arm A minus Arm C, using the per-item mean across the three seeds. A fifty-item contemporary set at λ=0.5 will watch style drift and dogmatic versus reflective replies.
